# lumbur puncture
- also called spinal tap involves obtaining and sampling cerebrospinal fluid from the spinal cord
- It is the gold standard diagnostic procedure in the diagnosis of encephalitis, meningitis, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and certain neurological disorders((multiple sclerosis and Guillain-Barre syndrome).\
- It is also used in the measurement of intracranial pressure and administration of medications or diagnostic agents
- The subarachnoid space is the area where the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) sample is obtained.

## contraindications
- skin infection near or at the site of lumbar puncture needle insertion
- central nervous system (CNS) lesion or spinal mass leading to increased intracranial pressure
- platelet count less than 20,000 mm3 (ideally the platelet count should be greater than 50,000 mm3)
- use of unfiltrated heparin or low-molecular-weight heparin in the past 24 hours
- coagulopathies (i.e., hemophilia, von Willebrand disease) and vertebral trauma

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A head computed tomogram (CT) should be obtained before performing a lumbar puncture if there is a concern for increased intracranial pressure.
>
> Signs and symptoms of possible increased intracranial pressure include altered mental status, focal neurological deficits, new-onset seizure, papilledema,
>
> immunocompromised state, malignancy, history of focal CNS disease (stroke, focal infection, tumor), concern for mass CNS lesion and age greater than 60 years old

## preparation
- spinal needle with a stylet (20 gauge or 22 gauge needle)
- four CSF collection vials
- sterile drape
- manometer with three-way valve
- local anesthetic
- syringes with needles (typically 18-gauge to draw up anesthetic and 25-gauge to inject into the skin)
- disinfecting solution (0.5% chlorhexidine/70% alcohol)
- sterile gloves, mask with face shield and surgical cap

> [!TIP]
> The use of a Whitacre spinal needle (pencil-point spinal needle) is recommended over a Quincke spinal needle (cutting spinal needle) as it results in fewer complications,
> As the Whitacre spinal needle is pencil-point and considered an atraumatic needle, the skin must be punctured before needle insertion.

## procedure
1. assess whether there are any contraindications to performing the procedure.
2. expalin and obtain informed consent
3. position in lateral recumbent position or sitting position

> [!TIP]
> The lateral recumbent position is preferred as it will allow an accurate measurement of opening pressure, and it also reduces the risk of post-lumbar puncture headache.

4. instructe p.t to assume the fetal position
5. The ideal insertion point of the spinal needle should be either in the interspinous area between L4 and L5 or L3 and L4.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> the speicfied intervertebral is due to the fact that cord ends at L1-L2 so inserting the needle below L2 will prevent cord injury

6. Palpate the landmarks before cleaning the skin are and before administration of local anesthesia. Once the interspinous space is palpated, use a skin marking pen to mark the area of needle insertion.
7. Wear sterile gloves and a gown, and clean the area with a disinfecting agent.
8. Allow the cleaned area to dry. Drape the area with sterile drapes.
9. Prepare CSF collection vials in order by labeling them according to the priority of collection.
10. apply a local anesthetic to the area of needle insertion
11. insert needle, CSF fluid will flow once the subarachnoid space has been reached by the needle.
12. Attach the manometer to the spinal needle by using a flexible connector.
13. Allow the CSF fluid level to rise in the manometer.
14. Document the level at which the CSF fluid stops rising.
15. Pulsatile variations may be present with the patient's respirations.
16. Normal opening pressure ranges between 6 cm of water to 25 cm of water.
17. After obtaining and documenting the opening pressure, disconnect the tubing.
18. Allow the CSF to drain passively from the needle hub into the collection vials.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Do not aspirate the CSF fluid by syringle, let it flow

19. collect approximately 1 mL per collection vial
20. replace the stylet into the spinal needle, and remove the needle
21. Apply gentle pressure with sterile gauze over the area where the needle was inserted
22. Cover the area with a small bandage.

## complications
1. post-lumbar puncture headache(most common)
2. bleeding, infection, spinal hematoma, and cerebral herniation

> [!TIP]
> Removal of the spinal needle with the stylet in place has been shown to reduce the incidence of post lumbar puncture headache.
>
> If a spinal subarachnoid hematoma develops, the patient may complain of acute back pain or new neurologic symptoms. The diagnosis can be confirmed with a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan of the brain
